# Whole-system and cross-block prior art: "one kinematic forecast, shared by both ends, drives frame design and receiver compensation"

Scope of this note: combinations of two or more blocks of the no-MAB design (README_nomab.md, doppler_link_adaptation_nomab.tex), the whole concept, and cross-domain analogues. The blocks are:
(B0) a free per-packet Doppler-scale measurement on data and ACK, with B's estimate returned in the ACK header;
(B3) a two-way Kalman filter fusing both ends on a drift plus damped-oscillator (swell) model;
(B1) a receiver that resamples along the forecast;
(B2) extra pilots placed where the forecast is uncertain, on top of a coherence-limited grid, plus packet length and modulation;
(B4) a threshold rule on forecast-predicted PER, with a margin calibrated from delayed ACKs.
Search date: 8 Oct 2026. Tools used: the local library (`~/Underwater/papers`, `tools/search.py` and direct SQLite reads of abstracts), WebSearch (standard and extended), WebFetch, and the OpenAlex API for abstracts. Claude in Chrome (Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore UI, Google Patents UI) was not used, so the patent and IEEE coverage below comes from web search plus OpenAlex only (see Gaps).

## Q1. Has the WHOLE system, or a UWA combination of ≥2 blocks, been published?

### Takeaway
No UWA paper, thesis, patent or preprint found closes the loop "Doppler measured on the link → forecast across packets → transmitter frame, pilot or packet-length adaptation and receiver prior". Two-way fusion of both ends' Doppler for the link (B0 + B3) was not found anywhere. The closest UWA works each cover one block plus a weak link to a second one: Doppler used as a lookup or AMC feature (MCS or FEC overhead only), Doppler estimates used for navigation (the opposite direction), or OFDM parameters (CP and subcarriers) adapted to measured Doppler spread without any forecast. A 2026 Acta Acustica survey of UWA communication–localization integration says the field is at an early stage and that deeply integrated architectures are still missing.

### Cited Findings
**UWA works closest to a combination (closeness ratings are mine):**
- **Park, Kim, Lee, Im, "Reliable Underwater Acoustic Telemetry for Ocean Remote Sensing Platforms: Channel-Prediction-Based Adaptive Polar–Raptor Coded OFDM", Remote Sensing 18(11):1747, 29 May 2026 (MDPI), DOI 10.3390/rs18111747.** Abstract, verbatim: "we replace the feedback step with a prediction step… with the Raptor overhead (OH) as the only real-time knob. The OH is picked from a lookup table indexed by three quantities the receiver can estimate online: SNR, RMS delay spread, and Doppler frequency. Two CSI predictors feed that table: TMSBL… and the Square-Root Unscented Kalman Filter (SRUKF), which tracks per-subcarrier variations." Pilots are fixed ("Pilot symbols on every fourth subcarrier"). ### Inferences
- The UWA literature splits into two directions that never meet: (i) communication → navigation, where the link's Doppler feeds a Kalman filter for position (Aubry 2019, Garin 2021/2024, Choi 2022); and (ii) channel/SNR → AMC, where prediction drives MCS or FEC (Radosevic 2014, Park 2026, Busacca group). The candidate claim closes the loop between them: a Kalman forecast of the link's own Doppler drives the frame and the receiver. This is a defensible gap, but reviewers will likely cite the Garin/Bouvet/Tomasi and Choi/Singer lines as "Doppler from the modem already fused by a Kalman filter". The paper should say plainly that those filters feed navigation and that neither returns the far end's estimate in the ACK.
- Park et al. 2026 is the most recent and closest "prediction replaces feedback" UWA paper, and it uses Doppler frequency as a LUT index. It should be added to the deck's "Doppler-indexed AMC" row; the deck already lists it, flagged "MDPI, not in library", so the full citation above can replace that.

- Not searched directly: Google Scholar, the IEEE Xplore UI, Google Patents full-text queries and ProQuest theses (Chrome not used). WHOI Micro-Modem, EvoLogics, Subnero, Teledyne and Kongsberg product documentation was not checked for vendor practices such as Doppler-dependent packet type or rate selection; that would need vendor manuals.

## Q2. Cross-domain analogues: a motion/sensing estimate → prediction → transmitter adaptation (pilots/frame/MCS) and/or receiver prior

### Takeaway
Outside UWA the loop is established, and parts of it are standardised. Kinematic tracking (EKF) driving both the transmitter (beamformer, removing pilots and guards) and the receiver (detector using predicted delay/Doppler with uncertainty) is published for V2I ISAC (Yuan et al., JSTSP 2021). Sensed position/speed driving pilot spacing is published (Sensors 2024). Doppler/speed driving DMRS density, midamble period and SRS spacing is patented and standardised in 5G NR and 802.11bd. Kalman-predicted estimation error driving greedy adaptive pilot placement dates back to Simeone–Spagnolini, ICC 2004. A June 2026 RF paper even models wave-driven buoy motion (Singer process plus current drift) in a state-space predictor for UAV–buoy ISAC beamforming. None found uses (a) two-way fusion of Doppler measured by both ends of a communication link, (b) a swell oscillator model of Doppler, or (c) a delay-tolerant acoustic setting where the forecast must bridge an RTT of seconds. So the novelty has to rest on these, not on "a kinematic forecast drives the frame".

### Cited Findings
**Large overlap with the concept (cross-domain):**
- **Yuan, Wei, Li, Yuan, Ng, "Integrated Sensing and Communication-Assisted OTFS Transmission for Vehicular Networks", IEEE JSTSP 2021, DOI 10.1109/jstsp.2021.3117404 (arXiv 2105.03125).** Abstract, verbatim: "relying on the estimated kinematic parameters of vehicles, the RSU can construct the topology of the vehicular network that enables the prediction of the vehicle states in the following time instant. Consequently, the RSU can effectively formulate the transmit downlink beamformers according to the predicted parameters… the vehicles can directly detect the information without the need of performing channel estimation. As for the uplink transmission, the RSU can infer the delays and Dopplers associated with different channel paths… inserting guard space as in conventional methods are not needed… an efficient uplink detector is proposed by taking into account the channel estimation uncertainty." This is the closest conceptual analogue: one kinematic prediction drives the transmitter (no pilots or guard) and the receiver (detector with uncertainty). Differences: RF radar echoes at one end only (not two-way communication-derived Doppler), no oscillatory motion model, no PER-threshold safety layer, and sub-ms latency instead of second-scale RTT. Rating: large overlap at concept level, different domain and mechanism. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03125); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1109/jstsp.2021.3117404)
- **Li, Gao, Zhang, Ye, Li, Yu, Wang, Xiao, Chen, "Sensing-Assisted Predictive Beamforming for UAV-Enabled Ocean Monitoring Networks", arXiv 2606.16717, 15 Jun 2026.** Verbatim: "explicitly accounting for wave-induced buoy dynamics… a correlated-acceleration state-space model is developed by combining a Singer process for wave-driven excitation with a slowly varying current-drift term. Given the resulting nonlinear reflection, Doppler, and delay measurements, the posterior Fisher information matrix and the corresponding PCRB are derived… A per-frame worst-buoy design is then formulated to jointly optimize sensing power allocation and UAV position." It is RF (UAV ISAC), not acoustic, and its decision variables are beamforming, sensing power and UAV position, not pilots or packet length. It is the only work found with a wave-driven drift-plus-oscillation motion model inside a communication predictor. Its Singer process is a correlated-acceleration model, not a damped oscillator at the swell frequency. Rating: partial to large overlap with B3 + "forecast drives transmitter"; different medium. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16717)
- **Yao, Wang, Zhang, Hui, "Adaptive Frame Structure Design for Sensing-Assisted Downlink Communication in the Vehicle-to-Infrastructure Scenario", Sensors 24(15):5061, 2024 (MDPI), DOI 10.3390/s24155061.** Verbatim: "the scheme leverages the vehicle's position data, as detected by the RSU, to calculate the real-time SER for DL communication. The SER threshold is set based on the requirements of DL communication. If the real-time SER is below this threshold, channel estimation becomes unnecessary. This decreases the frequency of channel estimation and frees up time and frequency resources… within the frame structure." This is "sensed kinematics → predicted error rate → threshold → pilot/CE interval in the frame", structurally very close to B2 + B4. Differences: RF, one-ended radar sensing, deterministic geometry with no Kalman uncertainty, and pilots are removed or kept rather than placed where uncertainty is high. Gain: "up to 6%" throughput. Rating: large overlap in structure (B2 + B4), different domain. — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/s24155061)
- **Deng, Deng, He, Lin, Wan, Wang, Feng, Fan, "Adaptive OTFS Frame Design and Resource Allocation for High-Mobility LEO Satellite Communications Based on Multi-Domain Channel Prediction", Electronics 2025, 14(19):3939 (MDPI), DOI 10.3390/electronics14193939.** Verbatim: "We utilize a LSTM network to jointly predict multi-dimensional channel parameters [SNR, Doppler, delay]… Based on these predictions, the OTFS transmitter performs two key optimizations: dynamically adjusting the pilot guard bands in the Delay-Doppler domain… and performing optimal power allocation based on predicted sub-channel SNRs." This is a Doppler prediction → transmitter pilot/guard adaptation loop, learned (LSTM) rather than kinematic, with no receiver prior and no two-way fusion. Rating: partial to large overlap (B2 driven by Doppler prediction). — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics14193939)

**Partial overlap: pilot adaptation driven by predicted error or Doppler (terrestrial, older):**
- **Simeone, Spagnolini, "Adaptive pilot pattern for OFDM systems", IEEE ICC 2004, DOI 10.1109/icc.2004.1312647.** Verbatim: "we explore the possibility to extend the set of transmission parameters to be adaptively selected to the pilot arrangement… Based on the prediction of the channel estimation error at the receiver, the transmitter can minimize the number of pilot subcarriers that guarantees a sufficiently reliable channel estimate… Prediction of the channel estimation error is performed by computing the error covariance matrix of Kalman filters. **Supporting theory relevant to R3 (pilot placement as sensor selection):**
- Minimising the trace of the Kalman error covariance is not submodular in general. Greedy bounds rely on weak submodularity or curvature (Hashemi, Ghasemi, Vikalo, Topcu, IEEE TAC 2020, DOI 10.1109/tac.2020.2980924). Trace minimisation of steady-state covariance is NP-hard with no constant-factor approximation unless P = NP (per search summary). Exact MIQP solutions exist for 30–50 states (Dutta, Wilde, Smith, CDC 2023, DOI 10.1109/cdc49753.2023.10383721). — [OpenAlex Hashemi](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Randomized%20Greedy%20Sensor%20Selection%20Leveraging%20Weak%20Submodularity); [OpenAlex Dutta](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=A%20Unified%20Approach%20to%20Optimally%20Solving%20Sensor%20Scheduling%20and%20Sensor%20Selection%20Problems%20in%20Kalman%20Filtering); [arXiv 1709.08823](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.08823)
- Lagona, Bockelmann, Dekorsy, "On the Bayesian Performance Limits of Pilot-to-Prediction in Frequency-Selective OFDM Systems", IEEE OJ-COMS 2026, DOI 10.1109/ojcoms.2026.3721910. The error is split into "temporal prediction and pilot-induced estimation components", a terrestrial counterpart of R1/R2's forecast-error vs. pilot trade-off. — [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=On%20the%20Bayesian%20Performance%20Limits%20of%20Pilot-to-Prediction)

### Inferences
- The headline claim "one kinematic forecast drives frame design and receiver compensation" is not new as a concept. Yuan et al. 2021 did exactly that in V2I ISAC: prediction → transmitter without pilots → receiver with uncertainty. Yao et al. 2024 did kinematics → SER threshold → pilot interval. Simeone–Spagnolini 2004 did Kalman error covariance → greedy pilot placement. The JSAC paper should cite all three and narrow the novelty to:
  - (i) the underwater setting, where the forecast must bridge second-scale gaps (2τ + T_a), and the swell-driven oscillatory Doppler is predictable for about 13 s while fading decorrelates in about 1 s;
  - (ii) two-way fusion of Doppler measured by both communication ends, with B's estimate in the ACK (G3; nothing found in any domain);
  - (iii) "shared by both ends" in the sense that the same forecast is held at A (frame design) and B (receiver). Only LEO/NTN has an analogue, and there the model is external ephemeris;
  - (iv) placing extra pilots where the forecast uncertainty peaks (zero crossings of d) on top of a coherence-limited grid. Earlier adaptive-pilot works change density or spacing uniformly, and Dong–Tong–Sadler show uniform placement is optimal for stationary models.
- R3's planned "greedy with a bound if submodular" is at risk. Kalman trace objectives are generally not submodular, so the bound will likely have to go through weak submodularity or curvature (Hashemi et al. 2020).
- The 5G NR DMRS-by-speed and 802.11bd midamble-by-Doppler practices are "reactive B0 → B2" baselines. A reviewer from terrestrial PHY will see them as obvious, so the paper should state that they are threshold rules on the current Doppler, not forecasts with uncertainty.

### Gaps
- Air-acoustic and in-body acoustic links: no specific search returned works with Doppler-forecast-driven adaptation. Coverage is thin (one general search).
- HSR: the found works adapt pilots to speed (e.g. "Adaptive Pilot-Assisted Channel Estimation for OFDM-Based HSR", Electronics 15(10):1991, 2026, MDPI); the full text was not read. ## Q3. Specific blocks paired: (B0+B3) two-way fusion; (B3+B1) forecast as receiver prior across packets; (B3+B2/B4) forecast-driven transmitter

### Takeaway
B0+B3 (both ends' Doppler fused for the link) was found nowhere. The nearest are the navigation filters fed by modem Doppler (one end only) and D-Sync/UWGS-type time sync. B3+B1 (Kalman Doppler across packets as a resampling prior) exists only within a packet or symbol stream: Kalman Doppler tracking symbol-by-symbol in spread-spectrum and FH-FSK receivers, and block-to-block Doppler prediction inside OFDM receivers. No UWA cross-packet kinematic prior was found. B3+B2/B4 in UWA was also not found; the only Doppler-to-transmitter uses are a LUT index (Park 2026) and numerology adaptation (Mangione 2021).

### Cited Findings
- A Kalman-based Doppler tracking algorithm for UWA spread-spectrum works "in a symbol-by-symbol fashion, using the time difference of adjacent symbols as input to the Kalman filter based on the delay-Doppler equation" (Applied Acoustics, 2021). This is within-packet tracking. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003682X21004680)
- Hu et al., "Iterative Doppler Tracking based on Kalman Filter for Underwater Acoustic FH-FSK Communication in High Mobility", MSN 2024. Within-packet tracking (already in the deck). — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11036394/)
- Koul, Hendeby, Skog, "Tracking Time-Varying Multipath Channels for Active Sonar Applications", arXiv 2602.15555, Feb 2026. An EKF on a wideband-Doppler-linearised state-space model "better captures channel dynamics induced by sea-surface fluctuations and transmitter and receiver drift". It is sonar detection, with no communication or transmit adaptation. It is relevant as a modelling reference for the swell plus drift state. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15555)
- D-Sync integrates the "Doppler estimate from the PHY-layer for relative velocity estimates with time stamps" for time synchronisation (two-way, but for sync only; consistent with the deck's G3). — [unipd networking chapter](https://signet.dei.unipd.it/underwater/media/download/378)
- Two-way ACK exchanges in UWA are standard for ranging: "The second modem replies to an acknowledgment (ACK)… calculates the acoustic wave propagation time". No work was found that carries a Doppler estimate in the ACK for link adaptation. — [same source](https://signet.dei.unipd.it/underwater/media/download/378)
- Li S., Wang Y., Wu S. et al., "Multi-body modelling and analysis of the motion platform for underwater acoustic dynamic communication", Appl. Math. Model. 109:455–472, 2022. A physical model of platform motion for UWA dynamic communication; the full text was not read, so its use for adaptation is unknown. — cited as ref. [54] in the [Acta Acustica 2026 survey](https://www.jac.ac.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.12395/0371-0025.2025322.pdf)

### Inferences
- G2 ("receivers re-estimate Doppler on every packet without a kinematic prior") still holds after this search, and G3 (no two-way fusion for the link) is the most robust gap: nothing was found in UWA, V2X, LEO or ISAC. The 802.11bd "mobility/Doppler level" in a midamble report is the closest thing to "Doppler returned in a header", but it is a coarse level that sets training periodicity, with no fusion.
- Li et al. 2022 (multi-body platform-motion model for UWA dynamic communication) should be read before submission. It may be the only UWA paper modelling platform motion for communication.
