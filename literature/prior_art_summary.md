# Nobody has closed the two-way Doppler loop

The searches found no paper, thesis, patent or preprint that implements the _nomab design in whole or in large part. Here the design means a two-way Kalman fusion of per-packet Doppler measured on data and on ACKs, giving one kinematic drift forecast that drives receiver pre-warping, extra pilot placement, packet length and modulation, plus a PER-threshold rule with an ACK-calibrated margin. The searches covered a 34,745-record local library, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar citation chains, Google Patents, arXiv and Chinese-language sources. No UWA work combines even two of the blocks. **Every block on its own has partial precedent**, though, and the headline concept "one kinematic prediction drives both frame design and the receiver" has a direct radio precedent in **Yuan et al. (IEEE JSTSP 2021)**. Covariance-driven greedy pilot placement goes back to **Simeone–Spagnolini (ICC 2004)**. The core of Block 3, "measure Doppler at both ends and send the far end's value back in the reply", was published in **DE-Sync (2018)** and **APE-Sync (2019)**, but only for clock synchronisation. The paper therefore cannot claim the architecture as new in the abstract. It can claim the underwater instance: a forecast learned from the link's own sparse, delayed, two-way Doppler samples, a swell-oscillator state that bridges second-scale round trips, extra pilots placed at the forecast's uncertainty peaks on top of a coherence grid, and a margin loop under action-dependent ACK delay. The current deck also has errors to fix. UWGS and geo-Doppler are one-way methods mis-cited as "two-way Doppler" under G3. Park et al. 2026 adapts only fountain-code overhead, not MCS. Abdelkareem 2015 already uses a previous-packet prior, which weakens G2 as worded. Two risks matter most for the theory: Kalman sensor scheduling is not submodular in general, which undermines R3's planned greedy bound, and a 2026 buoy field study found that finer Doppler tracking barely moved PER. Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore cited-by and full-text searches were not run, because Claude in Chrome was unavailable. The verdict therefore rests on OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and web search, and it carries a concrete list of remaining checks.

## The verdict: four blocks, no combination, one close radio twin

The question was whether any work implements the _nomab idea in whole or in large part. The answer is no on every axis searched. Each block note reached the same conclusion on its own: no candidate is "identical", no UWA candidate has "large overlap", and the only "large overlap" ratings go to non-UWA works that share a mechanism with one block (Agarwal–Honig–Ata and radar revisit scheduling for Block 2; Yuan 2021, Yao 2024 and Simeone–Spagnolini 2004 at the concept level). The local library sweep read the titles of more than 700 narrowed hits and the full text of the nine closest local PDFs. It found that "each ingredient exists alone". It found Doppler-rate models across packets ([Radosevic 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2013.2253212)), a previous-packet receiver prior ([Abdelkareem 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2015.05.011)), Kalman filters on Doppler within a packet or for sync ([DA-Sync](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMC.2013.13), [Hu 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSN63567.2024.00031)), motion-range packet design ([Zakharov 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2019.2932650)) and uncertainty-aware AMC with feedback scheduling ([Wu–Chitre–Anjangi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2025.3585657)). None of them combines these ingredients.

The UWA literature splits into two directions that never meet. In the first, **communication feeds navigation**: Doppler from the modem enters a Kalman filter for position ([Aubry 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/OCEANSE.2019.8867265); [Garin 2021](https://doi.org/10.23919/oceans44145.2021.9705890); [Choi/Singer RA-L 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2022.3154004)). In the second, **channel or SNR prediction feeds AMC** ([Radosevic 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2013.2253212); [Park 2026](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01747/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01747.pdf)). The _nomab design closes the loop between them. Two 2026 surveys back this framing. The Acta Acustica survey of integrated UWA communication and localization says the field "is still at an early stage" and that "research on deeply integrated architectures remains to be carried out" ([Shang et al. 2026](https://www.jac.ac.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.12395/0371-0025.2025322.pdf)). The IoT-J integration survey says localization, communication and control "are usually separately tackled" ([Yan et al. 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=A%20Survey%20on%20Integration%20Design%20of%20Localization%2C%20Communication%2C%20and%20Control)).

## Block 1: cross-packet Doppler priors exist only as heuristics or inside one message

Every UWA receiver found that predicts or tracks Doppler does so **inside one packet or one continuous message**. The Block 1 note concluded that no UWA receiver "carries a Doppler state across the inter-packet gap and uses it to pre-warp the next packet". The deck's G2 benchmark is Ceballos Carrascosa–Stojanovic, whose abstract describes "nonuniform Doppler prediction and tracking, which enable decision-directed operation and reduces the overhead" ([JOE 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2010.2052326)). The OCEANS 2008 version shows the mechanism is a block-to-block phase recursion in which the residual Doppler factor "is assumed to be constant during one OFDM block, but may change from one block to another" ([OCEANS 2008](https://doi.org/10.1109/OCEANS.2008.5151953)). Its prior is the previous OFDM block of the same packet. It has no kinematic model and carries nothing across packets (rating: **partial, closest in-receiver**). Two patents work the same way within a transmission. QinetiQ's EP1378078A2 updates Doppler "from immediately preceding sequence pairs (i.e. a closed loop system)" ([EP1378078A2](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1378078A2/en)), rated partial/tangential. Riedl–Singer's US9608738B2 tracks the time-warp sample by sample, warning that a constant-velocity assumption "is prone to instabilities" ([US9608738B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9608738B2/en)), rated tangential.

**The nearest UWA inter-packet Doppler prior is Abdelkareem's, not Unal's.** An earlier note credited the quote about the previous packet's acceleration setting the search range to Unal et al., ICC 2023. The gap check traced it to Abdelkareem's 2012 Newcastle PhD thesis: "In learning mode (LM), the acceleration of the previous packet is observed to designate an adaptive expectation range (ER), whereas the punishment mode (PM) drops an out of range estimation" ([Abdelkareem thesis 2012](https://theses.ncl.ac.uk/jspui/bitstream/10443/1443/1/Abdelkareem12.pdf)). The same work appeared as Abdelkareem–Sharif–Tsimenidis, Ad Hoc Networks 45 (2016, online 2015), which "accommodates an acceleration up to ± 1 m/s2 during the packet time" ([Khalifa repository](https://khazna.ku.ac.ae/en/publications/adaptive-time-varying-doppler-shift-compensation-algorithm-for-of/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2015.05.011)). The thesis also states that "the error in the OFDM symbol of index 1 comes from the Doppler variation of the last symbol in the previous packet". It fixes the packet design offline: "required Doppler resolution and acceleration contribute in determining the OFDM frame and packet length, respectively" ([thesis](https://theses.ncl.ac.uk/jspui/bitstream/10443/1443/1/Abdelkareem12.pdf)). Abdelkareem's prior is a one-ended gating window inside the receiver, with no state model, no Kalman filter, no feedback and no transmitter adaptation (rating: **partial for Block 1**, tangential otherwise). Unal, Enhos, Demirors and Melodia, ICC 2023, is a field characterisation of high-frequency Doppler plus a multicarrier estimator ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:Field%20Experiments%20Doppler%20Compensation%20High-Frequency%20Underwater%20Acoustic)), rated tangential. Its full text could not be obtained, so whether it reuses Abdelkareem's gating is unknown.

**Thales US 8,023,596 is an underwater acoustic modem whose predictor works within one message.** An earlier note called it a radio modem. The patent states that the modem "uses acoustic waves to transport the messages transmitted", that pilot tones are present "throughout the whole message", and that "the Doppler variation may be assimilated locally to a parabola", so the position "is predicted by means of a weighted quadratic regression" over the last T+1 measurements, one every 80 ms symbol ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8023596B2/en)). This is prior art for "predict Doppler from past measurements" at symbol scale, with a polynomial model, no feedback and nothing carried across packets (rating: **partial for Block 1 only**).

UWA Kalman Doppler trackers already use kinematic states, so **"Kalman with a kinematic model" is not new**. Sun et al. feed "the time difference of the two adjacent symbols" into a Kalman filter on the delay-Doppler equation ([Appl. Acoust. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apacoust.2021.108374)). Deng et al. track "time-varying Doppler effect and fast-fading multipath structure within a single frame" with Kalman filtering ([IoT-J 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/JIOT.2026.3681800)). Hu et al. state the scope limit outright: "Since the duration of a UWA communication signal is usually short, the change in relative motion … is not too drastic. Therefore the motion during this period is modeled by constant acceleration model or constant jerk model" ([MSN 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSN63567.2024.00031)). That sentence is useful for writing G2, because it shows the literature confines kinematic models to the packet on purpose. Related within-packet trackers, all tangential and all baselines rather than threats, include Sharif's velocity-plus-acceleration preprocessor ([JOE 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/48.820736)), Zakharov's multibranch autocorrelation ([JOE 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2019.2932650)), Cho–Kim's CP-based acceleration estimator ([IET Commun. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1049/cmu2.70136)) and Parrish's symbol-by-symbol Doppler-rate estimator tested at 0.29 m/s² ([WUWNet 2009](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145/1654130.1654131)). Further tangential within-packet trackers are segmented RLS per path ([Choi UComms 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/UComms56954.2022.9905690)), ADDC with per-tap DPLL ([Deguchi JASA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0040631)) and path-specific PLL/DLL beamforming ([Cuji–Stojanovic–Singer OCEANS 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/OCEANS58557.2025.11104574)). The last one supports the README's per-direction locked-path bias b_j, because it shows that different paths carry different Doppler. Yoshizawa's parallel resampler widens its range "by measured Doppler standard deviation" ([J. ECE 2018](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1155/2018/3579619)). That is a spread statistic, most likely from the current frame, so it is tangential. Zhang et al., OCEANS 2015, was flagged as a possible G3 threat because of its title, "Closed loop bi-directional…". It turned out to use a **bidirectional DFE** (forward plus time-reversed) with an in-receiver resampling loop, and nothing is exchanged over the link ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.23919/OCEANS.2015.7401933)). In Chinese UWA papers, "双向" consistently means a bidirectional equalizer.

**Cross-packet Doppler tracks exist in UWA, but they feed navigation, time sync or authentication, never the demodulator.** DA-Sync employs a Kalman filter "to refine the relative velocity estimation" for clock sync ([TMC](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMC.2013.13)). Ventura–Ardizzon–Tomasin predict position with "a Kalman filter or … LSTM" for authentication ([Access 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3651968)). Diamant–Wolff–Lampe track drifting nodes from Doppler ([JOE 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2014.2370911)). The VELP ([IoT-J 2021](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/jiot.2021.3094305)) and LSVP ([IoT-J 2023](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/jiot.2023.3311791)) Doppler-derived velocity predictors serve localization. Choi et al. use recovered Doppler "to estimate biases and drift from an inertial navigation system" ([RA-L 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2022.3154004)). In UWA, no paper or patent was found that feeds INS, DVL, GPS or a wave model back to the modem as a Doppler prior. The clean outside analogue is 3GPP NTN, where "the UE shall compute the frequency Doppler shift … and autonomously pre-compensate for it … by considering UE position and the ephemeris" and must "continuously update" it ([TS 38.300 §16.14.2.2](https://itecspec.com/3gpp/38.300/s/16.14.2.2)). That prior is deterministic external kinematics, not one learned from link measurements. Block 1's defence against "NTN pre-compensation plus a standard Kalman tracker" therefore has to rest on what is different: the forecast comes from the link's own sparse, delayed, two-way samples, it includes a swell oscillator with uncertainty σ_e, the receiver tracks only the residual, and the σ_e-to-PER mapping is part of the contribution.

## Block 2: covariance-driven pilot placement is decades old; the forecast source and the swell positions are not

"More pilots when mobility is higher" is old and standardised, so Block 2 must not claim it. In 5G NR, dmrs-AdditionalPosition adds zero to three DMRS symbols per slot, chosen by speed. Tomić et al. optimise this configuration across 40–500 km/h ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380828748_Optimized_DM-RS_Configuration_for_Improved_5G_New_Radio_Network_Capacity_and_Performance)), and patents configure "pos0… when UE speed is lower than a threshold" and "pos1-pos3" as Doppler grows ([US11923991](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11923991); [US12684534](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12684534)). NR's extras are uniform and set by speed class, but the structure matches Block 2's base grid plus extras closely enough that the paper must cite it. IEEE 802.11bd (NGV) goes a step closer. Patent text describes a midamble report that "may include at least one of a midamble periodicity or a mobility/Doppler level" fed back by the receiving station ([US11398886](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11398886); [US12047212](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12047212); which of the two carries the claim was not verified). That is Doppler fed back in a header to set training periodicity, a reactive threshold with no forecast. Qualcomm's Doppler-tracking SRS patent adapts measurement gaps by DCI, again without prediction ([US11943174B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11943174B2/en)). In the academic literature, pilot spacing follows fading statistics: a closed-loop codebook in which both ends "exchange information about the fading characteristics" ([Rao–Marojevic–Reed TVT 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.03176)); non-uniform frequency-domain pilots adapted by feedback ([Panah–Vaughan–Heath TVT 2008](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tvt.2008.2008655)); MDP pilot-pattern selection for V2X ([Yang et al. IoT-J 2018](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/jiot.2018.2867334)); training length and interval set by "Doppler frequency and frame length" ([Savazzi–Spagnolini TSP 2008](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tsp.2008.2009270)); velocity-set pilot intervals ([Hashida TVT 2022](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tvt.2022.3166639)); and channel-aging CSI update patterns ([Deng TCOM 2019](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tcomm.2019.2911575)). All are rated partial.

**The method of Block 2 has direct precedent.** Simeone and Spagnolini wrote in 2004 that "based on the prediction of the channel estimation error at the receiver, the transmitter can minimize the number of pilot subcarriers… Prediction of the channel estimation error is performed by computing the error covariance matrix of Kalman filters… a 'greedy' solution is proposed" ([ICC 2004](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Adaptive%20pilot%20pattern%20for%20OFDM%20systems)). That is Kalman error covariance driving greedy adaptive pilot placement as a link-adaptation parameter, for channel gains in terrestrial OFDM (rating: **large overlap with the B2 method, not with the forecast source**). Its 31 OpenAlex citers are all terrestrial. The closest is Šimko–Diniz–Rupp, who needed "only four bits of additional wide-band feedback per user… to optimally support RMS delay spreads up to 800 ns and Doppler frequencies up to 1200 Hz" ([ICC-W 2013](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/iccw.2013.6649217)). That figure is useful for justifying the ≈20 header bits. Agarwal–Honig–Ata track an OU fading channel with a Kalman filter and find a bang-bang training policy whose "switching boundary [is] defined in terms of the system state (estimated channel mean and associated error variance)" ([IT 2012](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tit.2012.2201345)), which is large conceptual overlap. Radar adaptive revisit is the closest structural analogue: "both the sampling interval and transmitting waveform can be adjusted according to the predicted error covariance output from IMM filter" ([Cheng–Zou–He 2013](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1049/cp.2013.0265); also [Zhang–Tian EURASIP 2016](https://asp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13634-016-0309-3)). It shares the method fully but has no packet, no pilot grid and no two-way fusion. AoI remote-estimation results, where "the optimal sampling strategy is a threshold policy" ([Sun–Polyanskiy–Uysal ISIT 2017](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/isit.2017.8006542); [Ornee–Sun ToN 2021](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tnet.2021.3078137)), are tangential to Block 2 and relevant to R2's irregular, delayed samples.

**Two 2024–2025 MDPI papers close a forecast-to-frame loop in radio.** Yao et al. use the RSU-sensed vehicle position "to calculate the real-time SER… If the real-time SER is below this threshold, channel estimation becomes unnecessary", freeing resources "within the frame structure" for gains "up to 6%" ([Sensors 2024](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/s24155061)). That is sensed kinematics, then a predicted error rate, then a threshold, then the pilot interval: structurally very close to B2 plus B4, but one-ended radar with deterministic geometry, and pilots are removed rather than placed where uncertainty is high. Deng et al. use an LSTM to "jointly predict multi-dimensional channel parameters [SNR, Doppler, delay]", and the OTFS transmitter then dynamically adjusts "the pilot guard bands in the Delay-Doppler domain" ([Electronics 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics14193939)). That is a learned Doppler prediction driving pilot and guard adaptation for LEO, with no kinematic model, no receiver prior and no two-way fusion (partial to large overlap). LEO practice more broadly is to "leverage ephemeris information to pre-compensate Doppler shifts, with the residual Doppler effect estimated by pilots" ([arXiv 2601.01956](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01956v1)). No LEO work was found that moves pilots according to ephemeris uncertainty.

**In UWA, G1 holds.** Zakharov et al. size the packet from a maximum acceleration, "Na > Θ fc aM/(2cΔf)", for a design with Θ ≈ 1.0 s ([JOE 2019](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8815825/)). None of its 26 OpenAlex citers adapts online. Hong et al.'s DSSS packet design is an offline rule from coherence time and bandwidth ([JASA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0037819)). Van der Werf et al.'s OTFS pilots "can be adjusted… to the delay and Doppler spread", which is a statistic rather than a forecast ([UComms 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/UCOMMS64662.2024.10847980)). Their journal version minimises tap MSE ([arXiv 2403.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19379)). Yang et al. adapt per-user pilot counts across OFDMA users, not in time ([GLOBECOM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/globecom59602.2025.11432537)). The closest UWA case of a frame adapting to Doppler is Mangione et al., which "calibrate[s] the prefix length and the number of sub-carriers" from measured delay and Doppler spread and falls back to JANUS/FSK ([Access 2021](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/access.2021.3082766)). It is reactive, works on spreads and does not touch pilots (partial). Two citing works found in the gap check are new and must be added. **Petroni et al.** predict CSI "through a Kalman-like filter so as to optimize communication parameters, including the cyclic prefix length… cast in a protocol where channel estimation/prediction are jointly considered" ([Electronics 2023](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/electronics12071552)). That is a Kalman-type prediction driving a frame parameter, but for delay spread and from one end (partial). **Kim, Park and Im** propose "a per-transmission controller that jointly selects the FEC scheme, code rate, and segment size from a prediction of the near-term channel state", find that segment size "is the dominant throughput lever", and replay the Watermark benchmark ([JMSE 2026](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/jmse14131239)). It predicts SNR and fading rather than Doppler, but it **pre-empts any claim that per-packet length adaptation in UWA is new**. In Wi-Fi, MoFA adapts A-MPDU length to mobility because "channel estimation conducted only at the beginning of frame reception [is] insufficient" ([US10477433](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10477433)), giving 1.8× throughput. It is the best terrestrial precedent for the N_sym dimension. Its conference citation (believed CoNEXT 2014) was not verified.

Nothing was found that places pilots at the zero crossings or jerk peaks of a periodic Doppler. Dong–Tong–Sadler supply the null result Block 2 can build on: for a stationary Gauss–Markov channel tracked by a Kalman filter, "single pilot periodic placement achieves the minimum MSE" ([MILCOM 2002](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1180512/); [TSP 2004](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TSP.2004.826182)). Non-uniform extras can only help because the swell drift has a deterministic oscillatory mean with uncertain phase, which makes σ_e²(u) non-stationary within the packet. This framing should be stated explicitly. Because the underlying fact is elementary (a phase error gives a drift error proportional to cos(ωt+φ)), the R3 claim must rest on deriving the optimal set and showing overhead savings.

## Block 3: DE-Sync already returns the far end's Doppler in the reply, but only to set clocks

**The two-way measurement exchange is not new, and the paper must say so.** DE-Sync (Zhou, Wang, Nie, Qiao, Sensors 2018) has the beacon record "the Doppler scale factor … obtained from the physical layer", then transmit "a response message back to node B at time T3, which contains T2, T3 and a_BA", after which node B records its own Doppler scale factor. The two directions are averaged: "v_m = 0.5 × (v_AB + v_BA) + ε_v" ([PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6021945/)). APE-Sync (IEEE Access 2019) keeps the exchange and adds a Kalman filter, but the filter's state is the **clock skew**. Doppler enters only as a per-round average, "θ[n] = 0.5 × (a_AB[n] + a_BA[n]) + ε", in the propagation-delay equation ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2912229)). DA-Sync (TMC 2014), which APE-Sync calls "the first time synchronization protocol that adopted Kalman filter to track the mobile nodes", filters Doppler-derived velocity and appears to be one-ended ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMC.2013.13)). TSMU/JSL adds IMM tracking for joint sync and localization ([MTU repository](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/11065)). D-Sync ([WUWNet 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1868812.1868815)), Mobi-Sync, DC-Sync ([Access 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3426357)), DAC-Sync, LT-Sync and MU-Sync are sync only. DE-Sync, APE-Sync and DA-Sync rate **partial**, the rest tangential. The citation chains (28 DE-Sync citers, 21 APE-Sync, 89 DA-Sync) turned up only sync and localization follow-ups, such as differential-Doppler TDOA localization and an EKF clock-tracking paper. None reuses the exchange for the communication link ([OpenAlex cites:W2804929802](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2804929802); [cites:W2941329658](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2941329658); [cites:W2141290731](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2141290731)).

**The deck's G3 citations are the wrong ones.** UWGS (Ghalkhani, Zhang, Campagnaro, Zorzi) explicitly **avoids** Doppler: "Unlike traditional approaches that estimate radial velocity using Doppler shift, UWGS utilizes multiple one-way pulse signals to establish a geometric relationship between node velocity and range" ([Asilomar 2024 PDF](https://signet.dei.unipd.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ASILOMAR_24-2.pdf); [ITU J-FET 2026](https://www.itu.int/pub/S-JNL-VOL7.ISSUE1-2026-A03)). It criticises two-way Doppler schemes for their signalling overhead. Geo-Doppler targets "a stationary source and a mobile receiver" and "achieves joint velocity-range estimation within a single transmission cycle". It is positioned against "traditional two-way ranging methods [that] suffer… due to motion mismatch" ([OCEANS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/OCEANS66983.2026.11617170)). Both are one-way methods and both are tangential. The two-way Doppler references for G3 should be DE-Sync, APE-Sync, DA-Sync and D-Sync.

Navigation is the other place where a Kalman filter runs on Doppler taken from communication packets. Aubry et al. have an AUV use "UWA communication decoding process to estimate both range and relative speed… via a conventional Kalman filtering" ([OCEANS 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/OCEANSE.2019.8867265)), one-way and partial. Garin et al. run an EKF on modem Doppler for localization ([OCEANS 2021](https://doi.org/10.23919/oceans44145.2021.9705890)). Further examples are an EKF on Doppler-derived velocity for trajectory prediction ([Li ICOSAAS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICOSAAS68663.2026.11649029)), OWTT with an EKF ([Randeni JFR 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.22133)) and LBL from Doppler differences ([Wu OCEANS 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/OCEANSE.2019.8867495)). None hands the track back to the modem. **No UWA work was found that puts a Doppler or velocity estimate into an ACK or control header for the communication link.** AMC and ARQ feedback carries SNR, CSI or MCS indices ([Stojanovic ARQ](https://millitsa.coe.neu.edu/publications/conference/arq.pdf); [UniPD chapter](https://signet.dei.unipd.it/underwater/media/download/378)). The user's own AoI bandit feeds back SNR context, not Doppler ([arXiv 2602.20105](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20105)). UWA ISAC receivers ([Men et al. TCOM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOMM.2025.3562328)) estimate velocity for sensing and feed nothing back.

Outside UWA, the closest work on the state model is **Li et al., "Sensing-Assisted Predictive Beamforming for UAV-Enabled Ocean Monitoring Networks" (arXiv 2606.16717, June 2026)**. It is "explicitly accounting for wave-induced buoy dynamics" with "a correlated-acceleration state-space model… combining a Singer process for wave-driven excitation with a slowly varying current-drift term" and derives a PCRB from reflection, Doppler and delay measurements ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16717)). It is RF ISAC, its decision variables are beamforming, sensing power and UAV position, and its Singer process is a correlated-acceleration model, not a damped oscillator at the swell frequency (rating: partial to large overlap with B3 plus "forecast drives transmitter"). It is the only work found with a wave-driven drift-plus-oscillation state inside a communication predictor, so the paper must cite it. Koul–Hendeby–Skog's sonar EKF, which "better captures channel dynamics induced by sea-surface fluctuations and transmitter and receiver drift" ([arXiv 2602.15555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15555)), is a modelling reference. Kim–Park–Yoo time sync messages to the turning point of periodic sea motion, measured with gyro and accelerometer ([KoreaScience PDF](http://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201317748766307.pdf)). It is the nearest precedent for the README's "quiet windows" extension, but it uses no Doppler and no filter. No work analyses **aliasing of swell Doppler under packet-exchange sampling**. R2's result, that halving the worst gap from 2τ+T_a to ≈τ+T_a doubles the no-aliasing range for an 8 s swell from 2.25 to 4.5 km, therefore appears unpublished.

## Decision rule: every part has precedent; the coupling does not

No UWA work combines a Doppler-drift forecast distribution as the PER predictor, packet length and pilot layout as actions, and a PER-target safe set whose margin is updated from delayed ACKs. The paper a reviewer will raise first is **Park, Kim, Lee and Im (Remote Sensing 2026)**, which "replace[s] the feedback step with a prediction step". It picks Raptor overhead from a lookup table "indexed by three descriptors… (SNR, RMS delay spread τrms, and Doppler f_d), driven by a CSI predictor that forecasts the channel one round-trip". It applies an uncertainty margin "δ = δbase + α·10 log10(1 + σpred/Ph), δ ≤ 3 dB" with a horizon "∆t ≈ 1.44 s at a 1 km range", and tunes the margin offline: "δbase = 0.5 dB and α = 0.5 were determined by sweeping… PER ≤ 0.05" ([MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01747/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01747.pdf)). Pilots are fixed on every fourth subcarrier. Block 4 differs in four ways: its margin is learned online from ACKs, its actions are frame layout and modulation rather than code overhead, its predictor is a kinematic drift forecast rather than a Doppler LUT index, and it uses ACK feedback where Park is explicitly feedback-free. Park's two citers are the Kim–Park–Im segment-size paper and an irrelevant FPGA decoder.

Wu, Chitre and Anjangi extend BER prediction "from a point prediction to an interval predictor… incorporating Gaussian process regression", and bound BER with the Quantile Absolute Deviation of "the predicted median BER… and the measured BER… from feedback" ([JOE 2025 PDF](https://arl.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Adaptive-Modulation-and-Coding-with-Feedback.pdf)). This is effectively a split-conformal upper bound with a static residual quantile. Physics enters only as constraints (CP versus delay spread, symbol duration versus coherence time), and a full-text search found no Doppler feature (partial). Block 4's "calibrated tolerance" should be positioned against it explicitly: an online, ACK-driven, ACI-style margin with a long-run PER guarantee instead of a static quantile. Radosevic et al. already model Doppler "during a period of time corresponding to a few seconds (or several data packets)… by velocity and acceleration terms which lead to a linear Doppler rate", and "linearly predict the channel one travel time ahead" ([JOE 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2013.2253212)). Their motion model only stabilises tap phases before the channel prediction, and the Doppler factors "are not needed thereafter" (partial). This work weakens any claim that kinematic Doppler modelling across packets is new in UWA. Its footnote, that one-travel-time versus round-trip prediction corresponds to "different functions performed by the two ends of a link", touches G3. Its 240 citers include no Doppler-forecast follow-up ([OpenAlex cites:W2024977294](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2024977294)).

Other partial precedents cover single parts. Chude-Okonkwo et al. adapt "the subcarrier spacing and guard interval" to receiver movement ([UJ repository](https://pure.uj.ac.za/en/publications/adaptive-transmission-technique-for-short-range-mobile-underwater/)). Anjangi–Chitre use Doppler spread to prune AMC parameters for a bandit ([UComms 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/ucomms.2018.8493161)). Wang et al.'s POMDP decides "to transmit or not" under predicted Nakagami–lognormal channels ([Access 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2017.2784239)), a structural analogue of the abstain option. Fu et al. choose modulation from "predicted data rates derived from the communication range" on moving platforms ([WUWNet 2019](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145/3366486.3366532)). Wang et al. allocate power with a Doppler-compensated ICI model ([MILCOM 2013](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/MILCOM.2013.95)). Ranganath et al. set packet size and modulation from a DRL state containing "Doppler characteristics" ([ICDCECE 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdcece68961.2026.11645748)), a low-tier venue with no forecast. On the terrestrial side, Belogaev et al. estimate "the strongest channel degradation at the time moment of the actual packet transmission" for URLLC MCS ([arXiv 1908.02227](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02227)). Brighente et al. pick MCS from predicted SINR statistics under a target BLER ([arXiv 2007.00306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00306)). Samsung's patent lets estimated Doppler set the SNR measurement interval ([US 8,654,822](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8654822B2/en)), and an NR-V2X preprint observes that SNR-only models "overestimate channel reliability" as Doppler-ICI grows ([Research Square](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-7192344/v1_covered_2ac9e13d-cd4d-418d-9642-047fe8cc7913.pdf)). That observation is the same physical motivation as SNR_eff = SNR/(1+SNR·φ).

**The margin update itself is OLLA.** "OLLA maintains an additive offset variable that corrects the latest SINR estimate to drive the long-term BLER close to the target value". SALAD "exclusively leverages ACK/NACK feedback" and outperforms OLLA but does not address feedback delay ([arXiv 2510.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05784)). Delayed ACI now has a coverage theory in which "the τ-delayed ACI recursion can be decomposed into τ interleaved ACI-like sequences" for a fixed delay τ ([El Halabi–Brandt arXiv 2609.07251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07251)). Conformal decision theory calibrates a decision parameter online from realized losses ([Lekeufack ICRA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA57147.2024.10610041); [Angelopoulos NeurIPS 2023](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-1000)). Three things remain unclaimed: the ACK delay depends on the action (it is at least h + 2τ + T_a, and T_a depends on N_sym), the setting is UWA, and the margin is coupled to a physics-based predicted PER. A web search for OLLA in UWA returned only cellular sources.

## What still looks novel, and how to say it

Six elements came through every search: (i) **two-way fusion of Doppler measured on data and ACKs, as an interleaved time series in a kinematic/swell state-space model, for the communication link**, which was found nowhere, in UWA, V2X, LEO or ISAC; (ii) the **per-direction bias state with path-switch gating**; (iii) the **sampling-gap and aliasing analysis** of swell Doppler under the data/ACK exchange (R2); (iv) a receiver **pre-warped along a cross-packet forecast with residual-only tracking**, so PER depends on σ_e; (v) **extra pilots placed by the time profile of σ_e²(u) on top of a coherence-limited grid**, clustering at drift zero crossings (R3); (vi) **a margin loop under action-dependent ACK delay**, coupled to a physics-based PER predictor. "Shared by both ends" has an analogue only in NTN, where the model is external ephemeris rather than learned from the link.

The candidate claim should drop any implication that "a kinematic forecast drives frame and receiver" is new in general. A defensible version is: *"Kinematic prediction has driven frame design and reception in radio ISAC [Yuan 2021] and Kalman error covariance has driven pilot placement [Simeone–Spagnolini 2004], but underwater links must bridge second-scale round trips with no external sensing. We build one forecast of the swell-driven Doppler drift from the link's own two-way measurements, fused at both ends, and use its uncertainty to pre-warp the receiver, place extra training symbols and choose packet length and modulation, with a PER safety layer calibrated from delayed ACKs."* The gaps should be reworded as follows.

## Risks for the theory and counter-evidence

**R3's greedy bound cannot be assumed.** Jawaid and Smith show that "the sensor schedule cost is not, in general, a submodular function, contradicting an established result", and give checkable sufficient conditions ([Automatica 2015 PDF](https://ece.uwaterloo.ca/~sl2smith/papers/2015Aut_Greedy_Submodularity.pdf); [DOI](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2015.08.022)). Greedy guarantees for minimising the Kalman error-covariance trace go through weak submodularity or curvature instead ([Hashemi, Ghasemi, Vikalo, Topcu, IEEE TAC 2020](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Randomized%20Greedy%20Sensor%20Selection%20Leveraging%20Weak%20Submodularity)). Minimising the steady-state trace is, per a search summary, NP-hard with no constant-factor approximation, while exact MIQP solutions are practical for 30–50 states ([Dutta–Wilde–Smith CDC 2023](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=A%20Unified%20Approach%20to%20Optimally%20Solving%20Sensor%20Scheduling%20and%20Sensor%20Selection%20Problems%20in%20Kalman%20Filtering)). For R3 this points to three routes: verify Jawaid–Smith-type conditions for the drift-plus-oscillator model, bound the submodularity ratio, or skip greedy for the single-swell case and derive the optimal extra set in closed form. The Krause et al. 2008 bound in the deck applies to Gaussian-process placement, not to sequential Kalman scheduling, so it does not cover this problem.

**Uniform optimality limits how much Block 2 can gain.** Dong–Tong–Sadler prove that periodic placement minimises steady-state MSE for a stationary Gauss–Markov channel ([MILCOM 2002](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1180512/)). Extras help only to the extent that σ_e²(u) is non-stationary within the packet, that is, when the swell phase uncertainty is large relative to what the tap-coherence grid P₀ already resolves. With a dense P₀ (short K_max), the extras may be few and the overhead saving small. The paper should present this as a precondition, not discover it in simulation.

**Field counter-evidence on surface buoys.** Over a 10.5 h, 26,170-frame surface-buoy trial, Lee, Park, Kim and Im found that "adding per-symbol Doppler/CFO refinement to the coarse compensation lowers the PER by at most a factor of 1.15… Per-symbol Doppler tracking is therefore not what limits reliability here" ([SSRN 2026](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7134057)). This bears directly on the deck's open question, "does a forecast prior still help acceleration-aware receivers?". On some buoy links the residual after frame-level correction does not set the PER, so Block 1 has to identify and demonstrate the regime where it does: longer packets, larger drift, fewer pilots, or stronger swell. The benchmarks should be acceleration-aware per-packet receivers (Zakharov 2019, Cho–Kim 2026) and symbol-level Kalman tracking (Hu 2024), not a constant-Doppler baseline. A third, softer risk is reviewer framing. DE-Sync's exchange, the navigation filters fed by modem Doppler, and NTN pre-compensation together make "close the loop" look obvious. The defence is that the contribution lies in the analysis (R1 predictability horizon, R2 two-way sampling and observability with per-direction bias, R3 placement), not in the plumbing.

## Conclusion

For this paper, the useful result is not just that nobody has done it. The underwater field has built every component of the design and pointed each one somewhere else. Two-way Doppler exchange went to clock sync, Kalman filters on modem Doppler went to navigation, cross-packet kinematic models went to stabilising channel prediction, and previous-packet priors went to gating the receiver's search. Meanwhile the radio world has already shown that a kinematic prediction can drive both the transmitter and the receiver. The contribution is therefore to re-aim existing machinery at the link under conditions radio never faces. Seconds-long, irregular, two-ended sampling makes the swell oscillator and the aliasing analysis matter, and the physics makes non-uniform pilots worth anything at all. That shifts the paper's weight onto R1 and R2 and onto a carefully scoped R3, which matches the deck's own ranking.

The two risks set the experimental agenda more than the novelty does. If Kalman scheduling is non-submodular, R3 should be proved for the single-swell case rather than leaning on a generic greedy bound. If the buoy study's 1.15× ceiling generalises, Block 1 must be shown in a regime defined in advance by long packets and strong swell, against acceleration-aware baselines. Clearing the priority-1 Scholar checks would turn the current "not found" into a citable novelty statement. The single most consequential check is a DE-Sync or Yuan descendant that applies the loop underwater.
